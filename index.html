Up to 40% of diabetic foot ulcers recur within one year.

This raises an important question. Are current approaches overly focused on closure alone?
Evidence reviewed

Risk of recurrence

Current evidence indicates that recurrence is not an unpredictable outcome. A meta-analysis on DFU recurrence identifies consistent risk factors including neuropathy, infection at presentation, poor glycaemic control, and prior ulcer history.*

This highlights the importance of early risk stratification in clinical decision-making.

Optimizing Wound Bed Preparation Role of Hydrogel Dressings

Wound bed preparation remains a critical determinant of outcomes. Evidence From 15 RCT on anaysis on hydrogel dressings reinforces the role of maintaining a moist wound environment.*

Evidence on hydrogel dressings reinforces the role of maintaining a moist wound environment and enabling effective autolytic debridement to support progression toward closure.

Adjunctive therapy in wound management

Randomized data from the meta analysis supports the use of adjunctive therapies such as negative pressure wound therapy, skin substitutes, platelet-rich plasma/fibrin, and hyperbaric oxygen therapy.*

Delayed escalation continues to be associated with suboptimal outcomes.
Timely escalation is essential in non-responsive wounds.

Role of AI in wound assessment

Post-discharge monitoring represents an emerging gap in care was highlighted in a multicenter proof-of-concept clinical evaluation done to assess the accuracy of an artificial intelligence system on a smartphone for automated detection of diabetic foot ulcers.*

Early real-world data on AI-enabled wound assessment suggests potential for more consistent tracking and earlier identification of deterioration.

Taken together, the evidence indicates a shift in DFU management. The focus is moving from isolated treatment decisions to a more continuous, risk-based approach that includes early identification, optimized wound preparation, timely intervention, and structured follow-up.
